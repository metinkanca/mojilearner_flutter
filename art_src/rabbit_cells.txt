# Rabbit cell map -- one character per art cell, built from the pixel-art
# reference roster (art_src/pets_reference.jpeg).
# Edit this file, then re-run gen_rabbit.py. Never hand-edit the generated SVGs.
#
#   .  background      K  outline        M  coat (main)
#   S  coat shade      N  nose
#
# The reference put the left eye (ours) one cell further from the nose than
# the right one. It is squared up here: both sit four cells out from the
# nose's centre line.
#
# The muzzle was off the same way: a two-cell nose over a one-cell philtrum
# and mouth gap cannot centre on this grid, and the mouth's left arm ran a
# cell longer than its right. Philtrum and gap widened to two cells and the
# arms evened up, so nose, philtrum, mouth and both eyes now share one axis.
# The nose then carries the muzzle rather than the philtrum: four cells wide
# tapering to two, over a single row of philtrum. Squaring it up with the
# small nose the reference drew left a heavy 2x2 black block under it.
#
# The face is pushed right on the head, as the cat's and dog's are. Measured
# against head width, the muzzle sits +10.4% right of head centre on the cat
# and +22.6% on the dog; here it is +12.5%, with the right eye 15.0% of head
# width in from the head's edge against the cat's 14.6%. The head outline is
# itself perfectly symmetric about cell 13 -- the face deliberately is not
# centred on it. Move eyes, nose, philtrum and mouth together, never one alone.
#
# Muzzle matches art_src/rabbit_ref.png, which is the authority for it -- the
# user hand-edited a render to settle the shape after several rounds of
# guessing. Three-cell nose bar, one-cell philtrum one row deep, then a
# two-plus-two mouth with a one-cell gap on the row directly below.
# Note the pixel-art reference (pets_reference.jpeg) drew a TWO-cell nose over
# a one-cell philtrum, which cannot centre: the nose overhangs a cell left,
# and that was the original lopsidedness. Three is the narrowest nose that
# centres on a one-cell philtrum; four over two reads far too heavy.
# from it than the right, which is the way the cat is drawn too -- its left
# eye is 36.5 units from its nose and its right 25.5. An exactly symmetric
# pair reads as too close on the left; do not 'correct' it.
